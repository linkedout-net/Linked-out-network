# Getting LinkedOut indexed by Google (and growing into a recognised brand)

Be realistic first: **nobody can guarantee rankings or "big product" status**, and you can't "apply" to Google. What you *can* do is make the site fully crawlable, tell Google it exists, and give it reasons to trust and surface it. The code side is done in this round; the steps below are what only you can do.

## 0. Prerequisites (do these before anything else)
1. **A real domain** (e.g. `linkedout.app`), not `*.fly.dev`. Brand searches, trust and backlinks all attach to the domain, so change it once, early.
2. Set `NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL=https://yourdomain` in production (`fly secrets set ...`). Everything canonical (sitemap, robots, Open Graph, JSON-LD) is built from it.
3. HTTPS everywhere, one canonical host (redirect `www` to apex or the reverse, pick one).
4. Make sure the production site is **public** (no login wall on `/`, `/stories/*`, `/companies/*`, `/layoffs`, `/circles`, `/decoder`).

## 1. Verify ownership in Google Search Console
1. Go to <https://search.google.com/search-console> and add a **Domain property** (covers http/https/www). Verify with the **DNS TXT record** at your registrar.
2. Alternatively add a URL-prefix property and use the HTML-tag method: put the token in `GOOGLE_SITE_VERIFICATION` and redeploy; the app emits the meta tag.
3. Repeat in **Bing Webmaster Tools** (it can import from Search Console). Bing also feeds DuckDuckGo and several AI assistants.

## 2. Submit the sitemap
- **Where to edit it:** the rules (which pages, priorities, limits, never-index list) are in `lib/seo-config.js`. The XML is generated by `app/sitemap.js` and served at `/sitemap.xml`. `app/robots.js` produces `/robots.txt`; `public/llms.txt` is a plain static file.
- Your sitemap is generated at `https://yourdomain/sitemap.xml`, referenced from `https://yourdomain/robots.txt`.
- Search Console, Sitemaps, "Add a new sitemap", enter `sitemap.xml`.
- Then use **URL Inspection** on `/`, `/layoffs`, `/decoder`, one story and one company, and click **Request indexing**. (A few per day is plenty.)

## 3. What to index, and what must never be indexed

| Index (these are in the sitemap) | Why |
|---|---|
| `/` Stories feed | Brand and category entry page |
| `/stories/[id]` (published, long enough, not opted out) | The long-tail engine: each story is a unique first-hand page |
| `/companies/[id]` **that have at least one story** | "what do employees say about X" searches |
| `/layoffs` | High-intent, news-adjacent, linkable |
| `/decoder` and `/decoder/[phrase]` | Evergreen answers ("what does 'we're a family' mean in a job ad") |
| `/circles`, `/circles/[slug]` (public) | Community discovery |
| `/translator`, `/title-translator`, `/premium`, `/terms`, `/privacy` | Product and trust pages |

| Never index (already `noindex` by header or metadata) | Why |
|---|---|
| `/admin`, `/api`, `/messages`, `/settings`, `/notifications`, `/profile`, `/my-stories`, `/alerts`, `/verify-employment`, `/login`, `/signup`, password pages, `/stories/new`, circle `manage`, company `business` | Private or useless to searchers |
| Stories with the author's "keep out of search engines" box ticked, stories judged easy to identify (anonymous + high identity risk), removed or draft stories | Protects people |
| Company pages with **zero stories** | Thin content hurts the whole domain |

## 4. Why server rendering matters (already done for the key pages)
Story pages, the decoder and the company metadata are rendered on the server, so Google receives real text and correct titles on the first request instead of waiting to run JavaScript. Check any page with **View source**: you should see the story text and a `<script type="application/ld+json">` block. Company page *content* is still rendered client-side (only its metadata and structured data are server-side). Moving the company reality summary to the server is the best next SEO upgrade.

## 5. Structured data (already emitted)
- Site-wide: `Organization` and `WebSite` with a `SearchAction` (sitelinks search box eligibility).
- Stories: `DiscussionForumPosting` (honest: a first-hand post, not a "review" or "rating"). Do **not** add `Review`/`AggregateRating` for companies; this product deliberately does not rate employers and fake review markup risks a manual action.
- Decoder: `FAQPage`. Companies: `Organization`.
- Validate at <https://search.google.com/test/rich-results> and fix warnings.

## 6. Technical checklist before you request indexing
- `https://yourdomain/robots.txt` loads and shows the sitemap line.
- `https://yourdomain/sitemap.xml` loads and lists story/company URLs (needs the database reachable).
- Each page has one `<h1>`, a unique `<title>` and meta description, and a canonical URL.
- Core Web Vitals pass in Search Console's "Page experience" report after a few weeks (images sized, no layout shift).
- Open Graph images render (`/opengraph-image`, `/stories/<id>/opengraph-image`); test by pasting a link into WhatsApp, X and LinkedIn.
- Mobile view works (Google indexes the mobile version).

## 7. How to grow from "indexed" to "known"
Search engines promote what other trustworthy places point to and what people search for by name.
1. **Be the answer to specific questions**: layoff tracker pages, "is X laying off", decoder phrases, company pages with real stories. Publish a monthly "State of layoffs" post from the aggregate data; journalists link to data.
2. **Earn links**: pitch the Layoff Tracker and workplace trend data to HR, tech and business reporters (Techpoint, Business Daily, TechCabal and similar for East Africa; Hacker News, Product Hunt for global). Offer a quote plus a data point, not a press release.
3. **Brand signals**: consistent name, logo and handles across X, LinkedIn, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube; put them in `NEXT_PUBLIC_SOCIAL_LINKS` so they appear as `sameAs`. Claim the Google Business-style profiles that apply, Crunchbase, Product Hunt, G2/Capterra later.
4. **App stores**: ship the native app with a keyword-rich listing; store pages rank in Google too, and `llms.txt`/AI assistants cite them.
5. **Content cadence**: a `/blog` or `/insights` with data stories ("Top reasons people left tech jobs in Q4") compounds more than anything else. Reuse anonymised aggregates only.
6. **Trust (E-E-A-T)**: keep About, Terms, Privacy, a contact route, a named team page, a clear moderation and right-of-reply policy, and a methodology page for patterns and the Reality Index. This product handles sensitive claims, so Google will weigh trust heavily.
7. **Don't buy links or mass-generate pages.** Programmatic company pages without real stories are noindexed on purpose; keep it that way.

## 8. Timeline to expect
- Days: homepage and a few URLs indexed after you request indexing.
- 2 to 8 weeks: most sitemap URLs discovered; impressions appear in Search Console "Performance".
- 3 to 6+ months: meaningful long-tail traffic if you keep publishing real stories and earn links. Brand-name results (your name at the top, sitelinks) usually follow once people search for "LinkedOut" and click.

## 9. Monitor (weekly)
Search Console: Pages (indexed vs not, and why), Performance (queries, pages), Core Web Vitals, Manual actions, Security issues. Fix "Crawled, currently not indexed" by improving content depth on those URLs and adding internal links to them.

## 10. Defamation and safety reminder for SEO
Stories about named companies are indexable. Keep the right-of-reply, report/contest flow, "N users report" phrasing and noindex-on-dispute workflow in place; a removed or contested story should drop out of Google quickly (use Search Console's **Removals** tool for urgent cases).
